### Summary & Key Takeaways
- **Vlog vs. Documentary:**
  - *Purpose & Content:* Documentaries focus on investigating or explaining real societal subjects (e.g., poverty, corruption), whereas vlogs share personal experiences, interests, and perspectives.
  - *Organization & Preparation:* Documentaries are research-based, carefully structured, and involve interviews and field investigations. Vlogs feature a conversational, personal tone focused on individual activities (e.g., cooking, gaming, tutorials).
  - *Evidence & Materials:* Documentaries utilize archival materials, formal interviews, and deep research data. Vlogs rely on personal experiences and direct demonstrations.
  - *Presentation:* Documentaries feature narrators providing objective, well-researched perspectives. Vlogs involve the creator speaking directly into the camera in a personal, informal manner.
  - *Vlog Organizational Structures:* Vlog content can be arranged chronologically (step-by-step order), as cause-and-effect, problem-and-solution, compare-and-contrast, or simple description/classification.
- **Literary Analysis — "Désirée's Baby" by Kate Chopin:**
  - *Author Context:* Kate Chopin (1850–1904) focused on women's lives, marriage, social restrictions, and identity.
  - *Plot & Themes:* Explores race, identity, social prejudice, class, pride, and antebellum power dynamics through Désirée, Armand Aubigny, and their baby.
  - *Irony:* Armand rejects Désirée assuming she has Black ancestry, only to discover later through a letter from his late mother that he himself is of Black descent.
- **Points of View (POV):**
  - *First Person:* Uses "I", "we", "my".
  - *Second Person:* Uses "you", "your".
  - *Third Person Limited:* Focuses on one character's internal thoughts and feelings using "he", "she", "they".
  - *Third Person Omniscient:* All-knowing narrator revealing thoughts and feelings of multiple characters.
- **Contextual Analysis Approaches in Literature:**
  - *Biographical Context:* Interpreting text through the author's personal background and life experiences.
  - *Historical Context:* Examining the historical events, time period, and era during which the text was written or set.
  - *Socio-Cultural Context:* Analyzing social norms, traditions, class structures, gender roles, and religious beliefs present in the text.
  - *Linguistic Context:* Analyzing word choice, sentence structure, diction, grammar, tone, and language patterns.
  - *Psychological Context:* Examining the internal thoughts, motivations, fears, mental state, and emotional struggles of the characters.
