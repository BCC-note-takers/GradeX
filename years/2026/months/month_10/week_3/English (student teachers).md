## Session: Informational and Journalistic Texts
- **Date:** 2026-10-15 (Day 4 - Thursday)

### Summary & Key Takeaways
- **Topic Overview:** The session focused on distinguishing between informational and journalistic texts, exploring their structures, features, and characteristics.
- **Types of Documentary and Informational Texts:**
  - **Documentaries:** Non-fiction films or videos about real people, events, or circumstances (e.g., science and wildlife documentaries).
  - **Editorials:** Articles expressing opinions, stances, or perspectives of media outlets or publishers regarding current issues.
  - **Interviews:** Conversations where reporters or researchers ask questions to gather facts, opinions, or statements.
  - **Historical Accounts:** Narratives that present factual, real-life information about past events and figures.
- **Key Characteristics:** Features of informational texts include real-life subjects, interviews, narration, statistics, authentic footage, and meaningful conclusions.
- **Modes and Styles of Documentaries:**
  - **Poetic:** Focuses on mood, tone, rhythm, and abstract visual imagery rather than a strict linear narrative.
  - **Expository:** Uses a voice-over narrator ("voice of God") and facts to educate or persuade the audience.
  - **Observational:** Acts as an unobtrusive "fly-on-the-wall" to capture raw reality with minimal interference.
  - **Participatory:** Involves the filmmaker directly interacting with subjects, conducting interviews, or engaging in the action.
  - **Reflexive:** Acknowledges the construction of the film and questions authenticity or the filmmaking process itself.
  - **Performative:** Highlights the filmmaker's subjective experience, personal story, and emotional response to construct a truth.
