## Session: Elements of Poetry (Meter, Talinghaga, Tone, and Theme)
- **Date:** 2026-10-10 (Day 6 - Saturday)

### Summary & Key Takeaways
- **Element 3: Meter (*Sukat*)**
  - **Definition:** The total count or number of syllables (*bilang ng pantig*) in each line (*taludtod*) of a stanza (*saknong* / *talturan*).
  - **Common Syllable Counts:** Standard counts such as 12 (*labindalawa*), 16 (*labing-anim*), 18 (*labing-walo*), or 7 (*pitong pantig* for traditional forms like *Tanaga*).
  - **Poetic Structures:** Stanza (*Saknong* / *Talturan*) and Line (*Taludtod*).
  - **Class Exercise:** Syllable counting (*pagpapantig*) per line using sample verses.
- **Element 4: Figures of Speech / Allegory (*Talinghaga*)**
  - **Definition:** The use of figurative or indirect language that imparts a deeper meaning (*mas malalim na kahulugan*) beyond literal definitions.
  - **Class Analysis:** Analyzed sample lines like *"Sa aking paglalakbay, ikaw ang sagwan sa aking buhay."* Students discussed how *sagwan* (paddle/oar) symbolizes inspiration, a partner, or parents guiding someone through life's challenges.
- **Element 5: Tone (*Tono*)**
  - **Definition:** The prevailing attitude, emotion, or mood conveyed in the poem (can be positive like joy/love, or negative like anger/sorrow).
  - **Class Application:** Analyzed a sample verse with a 7-syllable meter and identified the tone as negative and melancholic (*malungkot*), symbolizing heartbreak and isolation.
- **Element 6: Theme / Subject Detail (*Detalye*)**
  - **Definition:** The central topic or underlying subject matter (*paksa o tema*) of the poem.
  - **Class Discussion:** Noted that the sample poem's details center around personal experiences of heartbreak, isolation, or the loss of a companion.

### Summary & Key Takeaways
- **Pangkalahatang-Ideya ng Aralin:** Focuses on literary analysis of poetry, specifically examining the message and moral lessons of the work *"Ang Kwento ni Budol"* alongside the elements and points of view in poetry.
- **Classroom Dynamics:** Shifted from copying notes to interactive discussion, student participation, and **Sabayang Pagbigkas (Choral Reading)** where the class read sample stanzas together to practice proper tone and emotion.
- **Pagsusuri ng Akda: "Ang Kwento ni Budol" (May-akda: John Bixter Tino):**
  - **Maling Impormasyon at Penomenong Budol:** Explores the spread of fake news, scams, and modern societal deception.
  - **Pananagutan (Accountability):** Stresses taking personal responsibility instead of playing the victim.
- **Introduksyon sa Tula (Poetry):**
  - **Kahulugan:** Articulate expression of human thoughts, emotions, and experiences serving as a life guide.
  - **Pitong Batayang Elemento ng Tula:** Persona, Sukat (Meter), Tugma (Rhyme), Talinghaga (Allegory/Figurative Language), Kariktan (Poetic Beauty/Artistry), Tono at Damdamin (Tone and Emotion), and Simbolo (Symbolism).
  - **Unang Panauhan (First-Person Perspective):** The speaker narrates from personal experience using pronouns like *ako*, *ko*, and *kami* (e.g., *"Sa bawat pagbangon sa mundong ito, may ilaw ako, ang puso ko'y may pag-asa..."*).
- **Akademikong Terminolohiya:**
  - **Literary & Poetic Terms:** Panitikan, Akda, May-akda, Mensahe/Moral, Pananagutan, Tula, Persona, Panauhan, Sukat, Tugma, Talinghaga, Kariktan, Tono at Damdamin, Simbolo.

## Session: Ang Kuwento ni Budol: Summary, Disinformation, and Critical Media Literacy
- **Date:** 2026-10-06 (Day 2 - Tuesday)

### Summary & Key Takeaways
- **Summary of "Ang Kuwento ni Budol":**
  - **Character Profile:** "Budol" is a scammer and fraudster who uses a laptop and mobile phone to spread lies and fake news all day.
  - **Schemes Spread by Budol:**
    - *Raining Rice:* Claimed it would rain rice; people brought pots and basins to catch nothing.
    - *Bleach Cure:* Claimed drinking bleach cured illnesses, causing overflowing hospitals with sick elderly and children.
    - *Bridge Monster:* Claimed a monster lived under the bridge, but searches only revealed a stray cat.
    - *Alien Invasion:* Claimed spaceships were landing at the pier, prompting ship evacuations for a single pigeon.
    - *Election Disinformation:* A mayoral candidate paid millions to smear an opponent as an addict, successfully misleading voters.
  - **Climax / Plot Twist:** Fake news spread online that Budol had died (*"Patay na daw si Budol"*), drawing RIP comments, flowers, and candles. When Budol saw the post, he panicked and shouted, *"Buhay na buhay pa ako!"*, turning himself into an internet meme.
  - **Core Moral:** Scrutinize information carefully because social media can be deceptive.
- **Class Discussion & Key Takeaways:**
  - **Why People Spread Fake News:**
    - *Financial Gain:* Earning money from clicks, views, sensationalism, or being paid by politicians.
    - *Attention-Seeking (Pasikat / Main Character):* Seeking validation, clout, or playing the victim online.
  - **Class Examples:** Believing unverified class suspension announcements or falling for AI-generated images and deepfakes.
  - **Teacher's Final Advice:** Fact-check before reacting, and remember: *Think before you click, think before you share, think before you act.*
