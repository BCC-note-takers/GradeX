## Session: Narrative Point of View & Literary Context Analysis
- **Date:** 2026-10-08 (Day 4 - Thursday)

### Summary & Key Takeaways
- **Executive Summary & Session Overview:** Focused on Narrative Point of View (POV) and Contextual Analysis of literature (Biographical, Historical, Socio-Cultural) featuring student presentation (Arbaladja), teacher synthesis on cultural context and educational shifts, and a 20-item two-part evaluation quiz.
- **Narrative Point of View (POV):**
  - **First-Person POV:** Uses pronouns *I*, *me*, *my*, *we*, and *us*; narrator must be an active character in the story.
  - **Second-Person POV:** Directly addresses the reader using *you* and *your* to immerse them in the action.
  - **Third-Person Limited POV:** Uses *he*, *she*, *it*, and *they*; follows the internal thoughts of only one specific main character.
  - **Third-Person Omniscient POV:** All-knowing observer perceiving the internal thoughts, motivations, and feelings of all characters across multiple scenes.
- **Contextual Analysis of Literature:**
  - **Biographical Context:** Analyzing how an author's personal life experiences, struggles, and background influence their work (e.g., Kate Chopin, Edgar Allan Poe).
  - **Socio-Cultural Context:** Examining societal norms, cultural traditions, values, and behavioral expectations (e.g., wedding customs, greeting etiquette like *la bise*, handshakes, and bowing).
  - **Historical Context:** Investigating the specific era, political movements, wars, or historical events shaping the setting and thematic message.
- **Class Reflection (Generational Shifts in Education):** Contrasted past reliance on severe punitive discipline with modern child protection policies requiring mutual respect and clear communication.
- **Key Academic Vocabulary Glossary:** Narrator, Point of View (POV), Biographical Analysis, Socio-Cultural Norms, Omniscient.
