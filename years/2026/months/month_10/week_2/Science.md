## Session: Modern Biotechnology, Genetic Engineering, and Gene Therapy
- **Date:** 2026-10-10 (Day 6 - Saturday)

### Summary & Key Takeaways
- **Modern Biotechnology Goals:** Direct manipulation and alteration of genetic material (DNA, cells, organisms) in a laboratory setting to solve biological problems (such as diseases caused by viruses or bacteria) and produce desired traits.
- **Genetically Modified Organisms (GMOs / GMO Products):** Organisms whose DNA has been altered in a laboratory using genetic engineering techniques to enhance existing traits or introduce new ones (e.g., increased nutritional value, pest resistance, herbicide tolerance).
- **Local GMO Crops in the Philippines:** In 2003, the Philippines became the first country in its region to modify key staple crops including corn, eggplant, cotton, and rice.
- **Cry1Ac Gene:** Gene derived from the bacterium *Bacillus thuringiensis* (Bt) inserted into plants to enable toxin production that deters pests.
- **Bt Crops (Corn, Eggplant, & Cotton):** Genetically modified to protect against specific destructive pests like the corn borer and the eggplant fruit and shoot borer (EFSB) to protect crop quality and yield.
- **Golden Rice (Malusog Rice):** Genetically enhanced rice containing inserted genes from corn and bacteria to synthesize beta-carotene (Vitamin A precursor), aiming to combat Vitamin A deficiency, reduce childhood mortality, prevent blindness, and strengthen immunity.
- **Gene Therapy:** A genetic treatment method for hereditary or genetic diseases (e.g., hemophilia, cystic fibrosis, sickle cell anemia) aiming to modify a patient's genes directly.
  - **In Vivo Gene Therapy:** Direct injection or delivery of genetic material into the patient's body to target specific cells.
  - **Ex Vivo Gene Therapy:** Cells are extracted from the patient, genetically altered in a laboratory, and then reinfused or reinserted back into the patient's body.
- **Key Academic Terms:** Genetically Modified Organism (GMO), Cry1Ac Gene, *Bacillus thuringiensis* (Bt), Bt Corn / Bt Eggplant / Bt Cotton, Eggplant Fruit and Shoot Borer (EFSB), Golden Rice / Malusog Rice, Beta-carotene / Vitamin A Precursor, Gene Therapy, In Vivo Gene Therapy, Ex Vivo Gene Therapy, Hemophilia, Cystic Fibrosis, Sickle Cell Anemia.

## Session: Introduction to Biotechnology: Core Mechanics, Eras, and History
- **Date:** 2026-10-06 (Day 2 - Tuesday)

### Summary & Key Takeaways
- **Definition and Core Mechanics:**
  - **Biotechnology:** Intersection of biology and technology applying living organisms, cellular components, or biological systems to solve human and environmental challenges.
  - **Primary Biological Drivers:** Bacteria & Fungi (fermentation, enzymes, acids), Viruses (vectors for gene therapy/vaccines), and Plant/Animal Cells (culture for gene regulation and biopharmaceuticals).
  - **Molecular Building Blocks:** Plasmids (independent circular DNA vectors), Gene Segments (protein instructions), and Nucleic Acids (DNA/RNA).
- **Traditional vs. Modern Biotechnology:**
  - **Traditional:** Natural biological processes without altering genetic code (selective breeding, fermentation; e.g., cheese, yogurt, wine, *pagbuburo*).
  - **Modern:** Molecular biology and genetic engineering altering DNA sequences via recombinant DNA technology (CRISPR, gene cloning, synthetic human insulin in *E. coli*, mRNA vaccines, Bt corn).
- **Historical Timeline & Key Figures:**
  - **Ancient Eras (~6000–5000 BCE):** Unknowingly using microbes for bread, beer, and cheese.
  - **Karl Ereky (1919):** Coined the term *"Biotechnology"*.
  - **Paul Berg (1972):** Constructed the first artificial recombinant DNA molecule.
  - **Herbert Boyer & Stanley Cohen (1973):** Pioneered recombinant DNA by inserting foreign genes into *E. coli* plasmids.
  - **Human Genome Project (1990–2003):** Mapped and sequenced the entire human genome.
- **Practical Applications:**
  - **Medicine & Health:** Vaccines (triggering antibody production) and Recombinant Therapeutics (scaling human proteins using *E. coli*).
  - **Food & Industrial Processing:** Anaerobic fermentation and compound synthesis (e.g., citric acid production via *Aspergillus niger*).
