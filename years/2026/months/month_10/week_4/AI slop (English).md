## Session: Documentaries and Sentence Structures
- **Date:** 2026-10-23 (Day 5 - Friday)

### Summary & Key Takeaways
* Discussion on documentaries focusing on 65% facts, real events, and showing the audience the truth or the grand set of life.
* Detailed overview of the six types of documentaries: expository, observational, participatory, reflexive, performative, and poetic.
* Expository documentaries focus on a narrator with a hidden voice, behind the camera, guiding subject questions and providing facts.
* Observational documentaries observe the world around them, acting as an invisible observer to all sides of an issue without interference.
* Participatory documentaries include the filmmaker with a visible voice, who may be behind the camera or directly engaging subjects, incorporating personal accounts and experiences.
* Reflexive documentaries explore the process of filmmaking itself, revealing how the documentary was made and the filmmaker's experience.
* Performative documentaries feature experimental styles used to address grand subjects and ensure an emotional response from the audience.
* Poetic documentaries focus on visual beauty, rhythm, and tone, prioritizing aesthetic quality and mood over a straightforward narrative.
* Explanation of sentence structures, including simple sentences (one independent clause), compound sentences (two or more independent clauses), complex sentences (one independent clause and at least one dependent clause), and compound-complex sentences (two or more independent clauses and at least one dependent clause).

## Session: English 9 and English 10 Term 2 Prelim Exam Comprehensive Lesson Plan and Review
- **Date:** 2026-10-23 (Day 5 - Friday)

### Summary & Key Takeaways
#### PART 1: ENGLISH 10 EXAM POINTERS (pp. 71–86 & pp. 105–124)
- **1. "Désirée's Baby" by Kate Chopin**
  - **Author & Setting:** Written by Kate Chopin (1851–1904) in late 19th-century antebellum Louisiana, focusing on themes of racial prejudice, social status, and female identity. Set on the L'Abri plantation.
  - **Characters:** Désirée (abandoned child raised by the Valmondés), Armand Aubigny (wealthy, proud plantation owner who turns cruel), and Madame Valmondé.
  - **Plot & Conflict:** Armand falls in love with and marries Désirée despite her unknown origins. After their baby is born, Armand's demeanor initially softens, but as the child grows and displays mixed-race features, Armand accuses Désirée of concealing her ancestry.
  - **Climax & Twist Ending:** Heartbroken, Désirée takes the baby and walks into the bayou, disappearing forever. While burning her belongings, Armand discovers an old letter from his deceased mother revealing that *he* is of mixed racial heritage.

- **2. Narrative Points of View (POV)**
  - **First-Person POV:** Narrated by an active character using *I*, *me*, *we*. Offers direct internal insight but biased perspective.
  - **Second-Person POV:** Addresses the reader directly using *you*. Common in instructional media.
  - **Third-Person Limited POV:** Narrator is outside the story (*he*, *she*, *they*) and follows the internal thoughts of only one specific character.
  - **Third-Person Omniscient POV:** All-knowing observer perceiving the thoughts, motivations, and feelings of all characters.
  - **Third-Person Objective POV:** Camera-like narration observing only physical actions and dialogue without internal commentary.

- **3. Contextual Analysis Frameworks**
  - **Biographical Context:** Examines how the author's personal life experiences, struggles, and background shape the text.
  - **Historical Context:** Investigates the historical period, laws, political climate, and major events surrounding the work (e.g., slavery and miscegenation laws).
  - **Socio-Cultural Context:** Evaluates social class structures, cultural traditions, gender roles, and behavioral etiquette.
  - **Linguistic Context:** Analyzes diction, syntax, regional dialects, and vocabulary choices used by the author.
  - **Psychological Context:** Explores character motivations, subconscious desires, defense mechanisms, and behavioral patterns.

- **4. Informational Texts (pp. 105–124) — Vlogs & Documentaries**
  - **Vlogs (Video Blogs):** First-person conversational audio-visual media utilizing B-roll, jump cuts, and direct audience engagement.
  - **Documentaries:** Non-fiction films/videos presenting real events and facts (~65% factual core).
    - *Expository Mode:* Uses an omniscient voice-over narrator ("voice of God") behind the camera to guide viewers and present facts.
    - *Observational Mode:* Unobtrusive "fly-on-the-wall" perspective capturing reality without director interference.
    - *Participatory Mode:* Filmmaker directly interacts with subjects, conducts interviews, or participates in events.
    - *Poetic Mode:* Focuses on mood, visual rhythm, tone, and abstract imagery rather than a linear narrative.
    - *Reflexive Mode:* Explores the process of filmmaking itself and questions media authenticity.
    - *Performative Mode:* Highlights the filmmaker's subjective emotional experience to evoke audience responses.

- **5. Supporting Grammar: Sentence Structures**
  - **Simple Sentence:** Contains one independent clause.
  - **Compound Sentence:** Contains two or more independent clauses joined by coordinating conjunctions (FANBOYS) or semicolons.
  - **Complex Sentence:** Contains one independent clause and at least one dependent clause.
  - **Compound-Complex Sentence:** Contains two or more independent clauses combined with at least one dependent clause.

#### PART 2: ENGLISH 9 EXAM POINTERS

- **1. "The Cask of Amontillado" by Edgar Allan Poe (pp. 59–74)**
  - **Setting:** Unnamed Italian city during Carnival season; underground catacombs and wine vaults of the Montresor family.
  - **Characters:** Montresor (unreliable narrator seeking unpunished revenge) and Fortunato (wine connoisseur whose ego makes him vulnerable).
  - **Key Elements:** Relies heavily on an unreliable narrator, dramatic irony (the reader knows the trap while Fortunato believes they are wine-tasting), and the chilling contrast between the festive Carnival and the claustrophobic catacombs.

- **2. Literary Theory & Fairy Tales (pp. 75–86)**
  - **Patterns & Motifs:** Recurring symbolic elements, items, or narrative archetypes (e.g., three wishes, poisoned apples, hero quests).
  - **Sign & Referent (Semiotics):**
    - *Sign:* Combination of the signifier (word/symbol) and signified (mental concept).
    - *Referent:* The actual physical object or real-world entity being represented.
  - **Binary Opposition:** Dualistic opposing pairs organizing human thought and fairy tale narrative structures (e.g., Good vs. Evil, Light vs. Darkness, Wealth vs. Poverty).

- **3. Figures of Speech (pp. 42–58)**
  - **Simile:** Comparison using "like" or "as".
  - **Metaphor:** Direct comparison without "like" or "as".
  - **Personification:** Attributing human characteristics to non-human entities.
  - **Hyperbole:** Extreme exaggeration for rhetorical emphasis.
  - **Irony:** Contrast between expectation and reality.
  - **Oxymoron:** Pairing contradictory terms (e.g., *deafening silence*).
  - **Onomatopoeia:** Words imitating sounds (e.g., *buzz*, *crash*).
  - **Alliteration:** Repetition of initial consonant sounds.

- **4. Sonnet 18 by William Shakespeare (pp. 69–72)**
  - **Structure:** 14 lines divided into 3 Quatrains (4 lines each) + 1 Heroic Couplet (2 lines).
  - **Rhyme Scheme:** `ABAB CDCD EFEF GG`
  - **Meter:** Iambic Pentameter (10 syllables per line, alternating unstressed/stressed beats).
  - **Analysis:** Quatrains 1 & 2 compare the beloved to summer, highlighting summer's flaws. Quatrain 3 introduces the volta (turn) stating the beloved's beauty will not fade. The heroic couplet resolves that art and poetry grant eternal immortality to beauty.
