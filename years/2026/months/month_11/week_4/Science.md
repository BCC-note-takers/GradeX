## Session: Convergent Plate Boundaries and Subduction Processes
- **Date:** 2026-11-16 (Day 1 - Monday)
- **YouTube Link:** (link removed)

### Summary & Key Takeaways
- **Convergent Boundary:** A type of plate boundary where two tectonic plates move toward each other and collide.
- **Subduction Process:** When two plates collide, the denser plate moves downward beneath the less dense plate into the mantle, a process known as subduction.
- **Features Formed at Convergent Boundaries:**
  - **Trenches:** Deep ocean depressions formed when the denser plate subducts (e.g., Philippine Trench, Mariana Trench).
  - **Mountain Ranges:** Formed when continental plates collide and push upward.
- **Types of Convergent Boundaries:**
  - Continental-Continental Convergent Boundary
  - Continental-Oceanic Convergent Boundary
  - Oceanic-Oceanic Convergent Boundary

## Session: Plate Tectonics: Boundaries, Mechanisms, and Philippine Archipelago
- **Date:** 2026-11-17 (Day 2 - Tuesday)
- **YouTube Link:** (link removed)

### Summary & Key Takeaways
- **Review of Plate Movement Detection Methods:**
  - **GPS (Global Positioning System):** Uses satellites to track the continuous movement of tectonic plates.
  - **InSAR (Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar):** Uses radar imaging to detect ground deformation and surface changes.
  - **Seismograph:** Records seismic waves and earthquake activity resulting from plate movements.
- **Layers of the Earth & Mechanical Divisions:**
  - **Compositional Layers:** Crust, Mantle, Outer Core, and Inner Core.
  - **Mechanical Divisions:**
    - **Lithosphere:** Comprises the crust and the rigid upper mantle.
    - **Asthenosphere:** Lies beneath the lithosphere; consists of the plastic/flowing portion of the upper mantle.
- **Seven Major Tectonic Plates:**
  - Pacific Plate, North American Plate, South American Plate, African Plate, Eurasian Plate, Australian Plate, and Antarctic Plate.
- **Types of Plate Boundaries:**
  - **Divergent Boundary:** Plates move apart or divide away from each other, creating features such as mid-ocean ridges (e.g., Mid-Atlantic Ridge).
  - **Convergent Boundary:** Plates move toward each other and collide. Denser oceanic crust undergoes **subduction** beneath less dense crust, forming deep-sea trenches and volcanoes.
    - *Oceanic-Oceanic Convergence:* Forms trenches and volcanic island arcs.
    - *Continental-Continental Convergence:* Plates buckle and fold upward to form mountain ranges (e.g., Mount Everest in the Himalayas formed by the Indian and Eurasian plates; the Alps in Europe; the Appalachians in North America).
    - *Oceanic-Continental Convergence:* Denser oceanic crust subducts beneath continental crust, forming trenches and continental volcanic arcs (e.g., the Andes Mountains on the western edge of South America).
  - **Transform Boundary:** Plates slide horizontally past one another along fractures called faults (e.g., **Strike-Slip Faults** like the San Andreas Fault in the USA), causing friction, folding, and fault lines.
- **Geological Landscape of the Philippines:**
  - **Origin:** Began taking shape during the Eocene Epoch (56.0 to 33.9 million years ago) when the West Philippine Basin started spreading, forming island arcs that eventually merged.
  - **Tectonic Setting:** Sandwiched between the **Eurasian Plate** to the west and the **Philippine Sea Plate** to the east, both subducting beneath the **Philippine Mobile Belt**.
  - **Movement & Hazards:** The Philippine Sea Plate moves northwest at roughly 4–8 cm/year (while the Pacific Plate pushes west-northwest at 7–10 cm/year), causing constant compression, earthquakes, and volcanic activity (e.g., Mayon Volcano).
  - **Future Projection:** Over the next 50 million years, tectonic compression may cause some Philippine islands to merge with mainland Asia while others shift further into the Pacific, altering the archipelago's geography and increasing earthquake/volcanic activity.
- **Driving Mechanisms of Plate Tectonics:**
  - **Mantle Convection Currents:** Hot magma in the asthenosphere becomes less dense and rises toward the surface, cools, becomes denser, and sinks back down, creating a continuous cycle that moves plates.
  - **Ridge Push:** Newly formed, elevated rock at mid-ocean ridges slopes downward and pushes older, denser rock away from the ridge.
  - **Slab Pull:** The leading edge of a cold, dense subducting plate sinks into the mantle under gravity, pulling the rest of the plate along with it (coupled with slab suction).
