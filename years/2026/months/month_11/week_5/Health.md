### Summary & Key Takeaways
- **Core Concept of One Health:** According to the World Health Organization (WHO), One Health is an integrated and unifying approach to health recognizing that the health of humans, animals, plants, and the wider environment are closely linked and interdependent. Changes or pollution in one area (such as polluted water, air, or soil) can affect the health of others.
- **Three Foundational Principles:** Coordinating, communicating, and collaborating are needed to achieve One Health goals.
  - **Coordinating:** Organizing efforts and assigning clear roles/responsibilities (e.g., health workers monitoring situations, barangay officials organizing clean-up/prevention activities, and residents maintaining clean surroundings).
  - **Communicating:** Sharing accurate information, announcements, and updates (including via social media or community channels) so people know how to prevent illnesses and respond to health concerns.
  - **Collaborating:** Working together toward a shared goal (such as community-wide dengue prevention or clean-up drives).
- **Importance of One Health:**
  - **Preventing and Controlling Outbreaks (Zoonotic Diseases):** Helps detect, prevent, and control diseases that can spread between animals and humans (*zoonotic diseases*), such as rabies (from bites of infected animals like dogs) or dengue (transmitted via mosquito vectors).
  - **Ensuring Safe Food and Sustainable Agriculture:** Promotes safe, sufficient food supplies, proper food handling/washing/cooking to avoid foodborne illnesses, and sustainable farming practices without excessive harmful chemicals.
  - **Reducing Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR):** Encourages responsible/prescribed use of antibiotics and antimicrobials in both humans and animals, consulting doctors/veterinarians, and properly disposing of expired/unused medicines rather than flushing or throwing them improperly.
  - **Protecting Global Health Security & Disease Surveillance:** Protects communities worldwide from health threats and strengthens regular disease monitoring and surveillance across humans, animals, and the environment.
- **Short Quiz (1/4 Sheet of Paper):**
  - **Question 1:** Give the five common ways of transmission (*direct contact, indirect contact, vector-borne, foodborne, waterborne*) and explain how humans get infected.
